# Gluten-free South Asian: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams. South Asian home cooking is gluten-free by habit: rice, dal, besan, millets, jowar, cassava and coconut do the work, so nothing here stands in for anything else. The risks are the hidden ones: hing (asafoetida) is usually cut with wheat flour, and boxed spice blends and sambar powders can carry fillers. The tips say "check the label" where it matters, and hing only ever appears as "gluten-free hing (check the label)".

Places: Gujarat (kadhi and khichdi, tuvar dal with sambharo, guava chaat, shrikhand), Maharashtra (Kolhapuri chicken with jowar bhakri, egg bhurji, besan chilla), Tamil Nadu (ven pongal, idli with sambar, sundal), Kerala (kadala curry with puttu, sardine roast with kappa, egg roast with neer dosa), Karnataka (neer dosa, coconut chutney, kosambari-style salad), Goa (mackerel curry), Sri Lanka (salmon curry with beetroot curry, kale mallung and pol sambol).

Starches rotate on purpose: besan, rice (pongal, idli), brown basmati (dal, mackerel curry, biryani, a small plate with the salmon), jowar, rice flour (neer dosa, puttu), foxtail millet, cassava. White rice appears only in the pongal and the idli batter, in modest amounts.

Fish: Goan mackerel curry on Tuesday, Kerala sardine roast on Thursday, Sri Lankan salmon curry on Sunday. The three days are never back to back, and every fish dinner is cooked fresh, never a leftover.

Omega: the fats are canola oil, a little ghee and fresh coconut, with peanuts and cashews where the dish already has them. No flax, no chia.

## Checks against GUIDE sections 11 and 12
- Gluten: no wheat, atta, maida, sooji, barley or rye anywhere. No soy sauce. No oats. Hing only with the label note. Sambar powder, chaat masala and garam masala carry "check the label". Besan and jowar flour are bought from a gluten-free line if you are celiac (mills often process wheat on the same line).
- Not of the cuisine: no flax, no chia. Peanuts and cashews appear only where the dish has them (sundal, pongal, chutneys, biryani). Almonds are in the haldi doodh, the pongal tempering, the shrikhand and the biryani topping (a few, kept for vitamin E at the Light size). Coconut is used by the spoonful, as in Kerala and Sri Lanka.
- Renamed dishes: the biryani, the Kolhapuri chicken, the kadala curry and the Sri Lankan salmon curry say "inspired" where the cut, the bean or the method changed. Salmon is not a Sri Lankan fish, so that dish says so.
- Same flavour base twice in a day: coconut-chili masala is the Kerala dinner, tomato-onion masala is the egg roast or the Kolhapuri, never both on a day. Chutneys differ (coriander, coconut, peanut).
- One vegetable everywhere: kale is the green of the week (chilla, thoran, kadala, sambar, mallung), which is the one repetition left; tomato and onion are the cuisine's base and are not counted.
- Crutches counted: eggs in 7 slots, one a day, because the egg dishes are the cuisine's own (roast, bhurji, chaat, biryani topping) and they carry choline; dairy in 15 slots, never more than 3 in a day. Chilla, kadhi, chaas, raita and haldi doodh are the cuisine, and the Light size needs the calcium (pongal milk and the chilla yogurt are the slots that keep Light days above 80%). Peanuts in 8 slots, cashews and almonds only as garnishes.
- Lists match steps: raw chicken, fish, rice, dals, besan, cassava, beets. Kala chana is soaked on Thursday night and cooked on Friday for the kadala curry, with the rest kept for the weekend snacks, or chickpeas come from a can at about 2.5 times the dry weight. Fresh mint goes on the biryani.
- Missing staples: ragi (finger millet), bajra (pearl millet), makhana. Foxtail millet and jowar stand in. Kala chana is now in the library and is used in the kadala curry and the chickpea snacks.
