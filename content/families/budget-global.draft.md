# Budget Kitchen (budget-global): draft week

Cheap staples cooked well: dried beans and lentils, eggs, canned salmon and sardines, chicken thighs, frozen vegetables and fruit, oats, brown rice, potatoes and cabbage. Every dish is something a home cook in that country already makes. This is the final week, after the lab.

Places: Mexico and Tex-Mex (pinto bean chili), India (red lentil dal), Italy (sardine pasta with toasted breadcrumbs), Canada and the US (Maritime-style salmon patties, oatmeal, deviled eggs), plus a Chinese-style egg fried rice and an Eastern European braise (chicken, cabbage and mustard).

Carbs: oats, pinto beans, red lentils, potatoes, brown rice, whole-wheat pasta and bread. "Whole grains too".

Fish: Wednesday salmon patties (canned salmon with the soft bones) and Friday sardine pasta (canned sardines with the bones). Each makes two portions, so fish lands on Wed/Thu and Fri/Sat, and both dinners are cooked fresh. No premium foods at all (canned salmon is not on the premium list).

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Peanut butter and banana oatmeal. Mon, Thu.
- Berry oatmeal with yogurt (frozen berries). Tue, Sat.
- Scrambled eggs with spinach and onion on toast. Wed, Fri, Sun.

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: Smoky pinto bean chili on a baked potato. One big pot, 3 portions: Monday dinner, Tuesday lunch, and a frozen portion for Sunday dinner. Weekend project (Sunday prep: soak the beans overnight, 2 hours start to finish; pressure-cooker tip).
2. Tue: Chicken thigh traybake with potatoes and carrots. Makes 2, so Wednesday lunch.
3. Wed: Salmon patties with crunchy cabbage slaw. Makes 2, so Thursday lunch.
4. Thu: Red lentil dal with spinach and rice. Makes 2, so Friday lunch.
5. Fri: Sardine pasta with broccoli, peas, tomato and toasted crumbs. Makes 2, so Saturday lunch.
6. Sat: Mustardy chicken, potato, carrot and cabbage skillet. Makes 2, so Sunday lunch.
7. Sun: the frozen chili portion, thawed in the fridge on Saturday night.

## Fresh lunch
- Mon: Egg-fried rice with chicken, peas, corn and cabbage (brown rice cooked Sunday evening and cooled within 1 hour).

## Snacks (1 or 2 a day, Light keeps one on six days and none on Wednesday)
Peanut butter and banana toast with cinnamon (day 1 only), Greek yogurt with berries (twice), yogurt deviled eggs (twice), spiced roasted chickpeas (twice), peanut butter oat bites (twice), cinnamon yogurt with peanuts (twice), warm cinnamon milk, carrot sticks with spiced yogurt dip, yogurt bowl with toasted oats, boiled berries and peanut butter. Big adds one more snack on Mon (spiced yogurt dip), Tue (banana oat smoothie), Fri (warm cinnamon milk) and Sat (banana oat smoothie). Day 3 no longer pairs two banana and peanut butter snacks. No snack is cooked more than twice at any size.

## Sizes
Light 1600 covers 1400-1600 and 1600-1800. Standard 2100 covers 1800-2000, 2000-2200 and 2200-2400. Big 3000 covers 2400-2800 and 2800-3200. All bands pass.

## Checks against GUIDE section 11
- Not of the cuisine: no chia, flax or walnuts. Peanuts only in the fried rice and the yogurt snack, where they belong. Canola oil is the one cooking oil (cheap, and it carries a little plant omega-3).
- Renamed dishes: each name says what it is, and the "inspired by" lines own the departures (pasta con le sarde without pine nuts or raisins, egg fried rice with brown rice and chicken).
- Same flavour base twice in a day: yogurt appears in at most three slots a day on the plan as written (never four) and the spice bases differ (chili and cumin on Mon, paprika and turmeric on Tue, mustard on Wed, curry-style spices on Thu, Italian tomato on Fri, mustard and paprika on Sat).
- One vegetable everywhere: carrot is in the fried rice, traybake, slaw, skillet and one snack, each in a different role. Frozen spinach is in the scramble, chili, patties, dal and skillet, because it is the cheapest iron.
- Protein crutches: eggs only where the dish has them (scramble, fried rice, patties, deviled eggs). Yogurt is a dollop on the chili, dal and slaw, and a snack, never more than three slots a day.
- Lists match steps: raw chicken and raw rice and pasta, dry beans and lentils, canned fish.
- Batch staple: the chili says how many portions the pot makes, how long it keeps and how to freeze it.
