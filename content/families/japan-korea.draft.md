# Japanese & Korean Home Table: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, then revised after the lab and again after the chef critic. This is the revised week.

Places: Japan (teriyaki, miso salmon, saba shioyaki, cold and hot soba, onigiri bento, natto soba, nabe, tamagoyaki set) and Korea (banchan breakfast, doenjang jjigae, dubu-kimchi, bibimbap, dak-juk). Four Japanese dinners and three Korean ones. Both countries show up at breakfast and lunch too.

Carbs: brown rice and pearl barley, cooked in one batch twice a week and reheated (barley about 25 to 30% in the Japanese dishes, about 40 to 50% in the Korean ones, the way boribap is eaten). Four meals are not rice: cold soba (Mon), natto soba (Fri), hot salmon soba (Sat) and udon to finish the nabe (Sun). The plan labels as "everything in moderation" only because of the pre-cooked udon.

Fish: Tuesday (miso salmon), Thursday (saba shioyaki), Saturday (hot soba with canned salmon). Never two days in a row. No fatty fish is ever a leftover.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes, alternating)
- Japanese breakfast set: tofu and wakame miso soup, tamagoyaki, rice with a sesame, nori, bonito and walnut sprinkle. Mon, Wed, Fri. About 15 minutes with batch rice. The squeezed bonito flakes are toasted with the walnuts and sesame, then nori is crumbled in off the heat.
- Korean banchan breakfast: soy-braised tofu with gochujang, soybean sprout muchim, kimchi, a fried egg and rice. Tue and Thu. The tofu and sprouts are made once for the two mornings, so each is about 10 minutes.
- Dak-juk with walnuts, from cooked rice and barley. Sat, makes 2, so Sunday morning is a reheat.

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: Chicken thigh teriyaki with bok choy and carrots. 2 servings, makes Tuesday's lunch.
2. Tue: Miso-glazed salmon (broiled plain first, then glazed) with grilled king oyster mushrooms and sesame spinach. 1 serving.
3. Wed: Doenjang jjigae with beef, tofu, zucchini and king oyster mushrooms, over barley rice. 2 servings, makes Thursday's lunch.
4. Thu: Saba shioyaki, salt-grilled mackerel with grated daikon, spinach ohitashi and a wakame cucumber salad. 1 serving.
5. Fri: Dubu-kimchi, stir-fried pork and kimchi with seared tofu. 1 serving, fast.
6. Sat (weekend project): Bibimbap with beef, four namul and a fried egg, with a gochujang sauce. 2 servings, makes Sunday's lunch.
7. Sun: Chicken thigh and tofu nabe (inspired by mizutaki) finished with udon and a swirl of egg. 1 serving.

## Lunches
- Mon: cold soba with chilled tofu and edamame, with a ginger dipping sauce.
- Wed: tofu steak and small onigiri bento with broccoli goma-ae and a carrot and daikon pickle (everything but the rice balls made Tuesday night).
- Fri: natto soba with grated daikon, nori and a spoon of kimchi, with bok choy ohitashi.
- Sat: hot soba in a firmer ginger broth with canned salmon, bok choy and a soft egg (no tofu).
- Tue, Thu, Sun: leftovers.

## Snacks (2 a day, Light drops the second)
Mandarin with almonds and walnuts, Asian pear and walnuts, Asian pear and toasted almonds, mandarin and toasted almonds, soy-glazed almonds and walnuts (nut jorim), warm edamame, soft-boiled egg with nori, yaki-imo from the Sunday tray (Mon and Wed), cucumber and carrot sticks with miso dip, oi-muchim. Big adds hiyayakko (Mon, Thu), mandarin and walnuts (Tue, Sun), a soft egg with nori (Wed), warm edamame (Fri) and mandarin and almonds (Sat).

## Check against GUIDE section 11
- Not of the cuisine: nothing added only for a number. The ground flax furikake is gone. Walnuts belong in both cuisines (hodu-juk, nut jorim, kurumi). Almonds appear in nut jorim and with fruit, to carry vitamin E. Miso and doenjang, gochujang and doenjang are the real pastes.
- Renamed dishes: jjigae, bibimbap and the nabe say "inspired by" where they depart from the original, and name what changed.
- Protein crutches: eggs now appear in the breakfast set, banchan breakfast, bibimbap, hot soba (tsukimi style) and the udon finish, plus one egg snack. All are real parts of those dishes. The soba, jjigae and juk eggs are gone.
- Same flavour base twice in a day: breakfast alternates, so the miso soup set and the Korean banchan never repeat on consecutive days. Wednesday has miso soup at breakfast and a doenjang stew at dinner, which is two different pastes from two cuisines.
- One vegetable everywhere: spinach is in 4 recipes (gomae, saba ohitashi, bento, bibimbap) and in the nabe, never twice in a day except Sunday's leftover bibimbap plus nabe. Mushrooms rotate between king oyster, shiitake and none. The fruit is mandarin and Asian pear.
- Weird breakfasts: none.
- Lists match steps: all grain is the cooked batch ("from your batch"), and everything else cooked is raw. Canned salmon, frozen edamame, natto and pre-cooked udon are the only ready foods.
- Untrue claims: calcium claims are only made for dishes with calcium-set tofu, and those ingredients say "check the label for calcium sulfate".
- Silent sub-recipes: the dipping sauces, ponzu, miso glaze, gochujang sauce and the sprinkle are made inside the steps. The rice batch is explained in the Sunday prep paragraph.

## What changed after the final review
Snacks on D1 and D3 now use the Sunday yaki-imo, the hot soba lost its tofu and got a stronger broth, the nabe ponzu is sharp (mandarin, vinegar, tamari, grated daikon and scallion), thin broths are shorter, the bento greens are broccoli goma-ae, bibimbap has sesame seeds on top, soba portions are bigger so Light still looks like noodles, and gochugaru is back in the banchan, jjigae and oi-muchim.

## What changed after the critic (fix numbers)
See the hand-back changelog. The big moves: flax out, chicken thigh back, rice batch-cooked with barley, the banchan breakfast batched and alternating, gochujang and doenjang as real ingredients, four non-rice meals, spinach and eggs spread out, and the chazuke replaced with a hot soba.
