# Vegan Mediterranean & Levant: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, then revised after the lab, the engine fixes (plain soy yogurt and seitan now count) and the chef critic's report. This is the final week.

Places: Lebanon (mujaddara hamra, hummus bowl, shawarma plate, falafel, adas bi hamod, fatteh, lupini), Syria (muhammara, the figs and nuts plate), Greece (gigantes plaki, horiatiki with tofu "feta", the Lenten chard pie), Egypt (ful medames, fig shakes, falafel with fava, roasted peanuts) and Sicily (caponata with white beans).

Carbs: lentils, beans, chickpeas, bulgur, oats and whole-wheat pita. The week is labelled "everything in moderation" only because of the phyllo in the Lenten pie. No rice, no pasta, no potatoes.

Fortified B12: a soy beverage with real B12 (original, vitamins A and D) goes into the oats, the fig shake or a glass beside the fatteh, so B12 is covered every day. The library record carries the US level (about 1 µg per cup), and many Canadian cartons carry less, so `howItWorks` says to check the carton and treats a B12 supplement as the safe bet. Plain soy yogurt has no added B12, so it is never counted for it.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Overnight oats with soy beverage, ground flax, wheat germ, walnuts and figs. Mon, Thu, Fri. About three-quarters of the soy beverage goes in the jar and the rest is a glass alongside.
- Chickpea fatteh with garlicky soy yogurt, tahini and walnuts. Tue, Sun (fatteh is traditionally dressed with yogurt, so plain soy yogurt is the natural plant-based swap; hemp hearts are blended into the sauce).
- Ful medames with lemon, tomato and parsley (oil, lemon and cumin, no tahini, as in Cairo). Wed, Sat (the fig, flax and wheat germ shake is the soy that day).
- Light only: Mon and Fri swap the oats for the shake, and Sat swaps the ful for the oats, so the shake never lands on two days in a row.

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: Mujaddara hamra with caramelized onions and malfouf salad, lentils and coarse bulgur in near-equal parts. Makes Tuesday's lunch.
2. Tue: Gigantes plaki, giant white beans baked in tomato and dill. Makes Wednesday's lunch.
3. Wed: Seitan, mushroom and cauliflower shawarma plate with a tofu garlic sauce, pickled carrot and warm pita. One serving, roasted on one tray.
4. Thu: Adas bi hamod, lemony lentil and chard soup with toasted pita. Makes Friday's lunch.
5. Fri: Baked chickpea and fava falafel with roasted cauliflower and tahini sauce. Makes Saturday's lunch.
6. Sat (weekend project): Lenten chard and mushroom pie with calcium-set tofu "feta" (a hortopita and manitaropita hybrid). Makes Sunday's lunch.
7. Sun: Sicilian caponata over white beans and chickpeas. One serving. It keeps 4 days, so it can be made on Saturday.

## Lunches

- Thu: Horiatiki with tofu "feta" marinated for a few hours, and white beans.
- Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat, Sun: leftovers.

Big adds hummus with carrot and cucumber, seitan bites, tawook skewers, lupini and dried figs with almonds.

## Check against GUIDE sections 11 and 12
- Tofu: 5 slots (hummus bowl, horiatiki, shawarma sauce, and the pie twice, all calcium-set). Seitan is 4 main and snack slots (hummus bowl, shawarma plate, tawook skewers, kafta skewers). Lentils, white beans, chickpeas, fava, lupini and edamame fill the rest, so tofu and chickpeas do not carry the week.
- Fortified soy beverage: 7 slots, one a day, in the oats (3), the shake (2) and beside the fatteh (2). Plain soy yogurt: 2 slots (the fatteh).
- Nuts and seeds: walnuts in 7 slots, almonds in 2, flax in 5, hemp in 2 (fatteh sauce), wheat germ in 5, tahini in 5, peanuts in 1.
- Cauliflower is in 4 slots (hummus bowl, shawarma plate, falafel and its leftover), only where roasted cauliflower is a real Levantine pairing.
- No chia, no sunflower or corn oil. Canola only for neutral roasting, frying and brushing phyllo (as on Greek fasting days). Olive oil for dressings and sauces.
- No animal foods, no honey. Sweetness comes from figs and pomegranate molasses.
- Iodized salt is the only salt, with enough of it for the dry pulses.
- Unusual for the cuisine: wheat germ in the oats and shake, hemp hearts in the fatteh sauce, and edamame in place of fresh green fava. All are noted as modern tweaks.

## What changed and why
- Koshari, louvia and a date shake were drafted and cut. Koshari and rice dishes drove the glycemic load up and the protein share down, and the grocery list went over 45.
- After the engine fixes: fatteh went back to its yogurt-style sauce (soy yogurt), which cut oats to three mornings. Seitan joined the hummus bowl, which keeps calcium under the upper limit on Big days.
- After the chef critic: the shawarma became a plate; ful lost its tahini and got more fava; cauliflower came out of the mujaddara and horiatiki; the oats jar lost its hemp and gained walnuts and figs; the pie, falafel, caponata and hummus bowl got more oil and salt; the hummus bowl, gigantes and caponata lost their crumb-sized pita (bread on the side is optional).
- Light is back at 1600-1800 with a 1700 nominal. The 1400-1600 band still cannot be claimed: at 1,400 kcal five of the seven days cannot be trimmed within 5% of the target without dropping a protected nutrient under 80%. Big covers 2400-3200.
