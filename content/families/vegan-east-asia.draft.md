# Vegan East Asian: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Places: China (Taiwanese dou jiang, Sichuan mapo tofu, Cantonese black bean stir-fry, Chinese congee and almond and black sesame drinks), Japan (natto and miso breakfast, soba, teriyaki, tonyu nabe), Korea (sundubu jjigae, bibimbap, goguma latte) and Vietnam (bún chay).

Carbs: brown rice is the everyday grain (cooked once on Sunday, about 2½ cups dry, kept 4 or 5 days, the rest frozen). Soba, rice vermicelli and a little whole-wheat toast are the only other starches, so the family is labelled "everything in moderation" because of the vermicelli and toast.

Fortified B12: every day has fortified soy milk, at least a glass and usually two. The numbers assume the library's carton (about 2 µg per cup). `howItWorks` says that plainly, that many Canadian cartons carry about half, and that a daily B12 supplement is the safe bet. Plain soy yogurt is not used, and nothing else is counted for B12 (not nori, not soba).

Foods kept out on purpose: kombu (the library's dried kombu carries several times the iodine upper limit in a gram or two), fish sauce and oyster sauce (a soy nước chấm stands in), dashi from bonito (shiitake dashi instead), honey, eggs. Only a pinch of wakame, in one breakfast. Salt is iodized. Kimchi is labelled "vegan, check the label".

Omega-3: ALA comes from ground flax in four of the warm soy milk drinks and the congee, with canola as the cooking oil. Wheat germ and walnuts are out, and flax is no longer in the soba dressing or the nabe dip. Sesame oil is a finishing drizzle (1 to 6 g) and fries the kimchi in the jjigae. Walnuts are left out to keep the shopping list under 45 groceries.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Taiwanese xian dou jiang: hot soy milk curdled with rice vinegar, a few cubes of soft tofu, peanuts, scallion, quick chili crisp and toast cubes. Mon, Wed, Fri.
- Japanese breakfast: natto over rice, miso soup with soft tofu and shiitake dashi (soaked overnight), sesame spinach. Tue, Thu.
- Soy milk and mung bean congee with shiitake, edamame and almonds, flax stirred in (weekend project, 80 minutes, makes two). Sat, Sun (leftover).

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: Mapo tofu with mushrooms and soy mince, garlicky cauliflower, brown rice. Makes Tuesday's lunch.
2. Tue: Lemongrass soya chunk and tofu bún chay, a cool herby bowl (moved up so the two red stews are not on the same day). Makes Wednesday's lunch.
3. Wed: Sundubu jjigae with soft tofu, vegan kimchi, bok choy and soybean sprouts. Makes Thursday's lunch.
4. Thu: Tempeh and broccoli stir-fry with black beans and cauliflower. Makes Friday's lunch.
5. Fri: Seitan teriyaki with oyster mushrooms, edamame, simmered sweet potato and bok choy. Makes Saturday's lunch.
6. Sat (weekend project): Bibimbap with crispy tofu, tempeh bulgogi and four namul. Makes Sunday's lunch.
7. Sun: Tonyu nabe (soy milk hot pot) with soft tofu, edamame, napa, carrot and shiitake, and a goma dare dip. One serving; finish with rice as zosui.

## Lunches
- Mon: Edamame and soba salad with a goma-ae style sesame-miso dressing (edamame is the protein).
- Tue to Sun: leftovers.

## Snacks (one or two a day)

- Thu: ginger soy milk blended with almonds and flax; roasted soybeans with a mandarin.
- Fri: black sesame, almond and kinako soy milk; chilled tofu with ginger.
- Sat: kinako and almond soy latte (dropped at Light).

## Bands
- Light claims 1600-1800 only. The 1400-1600 band was tried: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday would not trim below about 1480 to 1560 kcal without dropping vitamin E, choline or B12 under 80%, because the soy milk drinks, almonds and mushrooms that carry them are the day.

## Chef critic round (fixes 1 to 15)
Applied: the drinks are real drinks (black sesame with kinako, kinako, ginger, goguma; no wheat germ), day 2 and 3 stews swapped, sundubu uses real soft tofu (the soft tofu entry is used for the jjigae, dou jiang, miso soup and nabe), teriyaki and bibimbap and bún chay re-cooked, congee cooks 70 minutes, soba salad no longer has soybeans or flax, Friday snack is salted edamame, text and labels cleaned.
Deviations to keep the gate: canola in the mapo is 12 g (14 g breaks the 27 g oil cap at Big); the sundubu keeps 90 g edamame and the teriyaki 110 g, and the ginger and goguma drinks keep a little flax (6 to 10 g), because choline and the daily omega ratio fail without them; mirin and lemon were not added (each would be a 46th grocery), so teriyaki and nước chấm use sugar and rice vinegar.

## Check against GUIDE sections 11 and 12
- Protein: tofu in 14 slots (at most 3 in a day), tempeh 4, natto 2, edamame 10, mung beans 2, roasted soybeans 4, seitan 2, soya chunks 4. Every lunch and dinner has 15 g or more from real protein sources at every size.
- Fortified soy milk: 12 slots, every day, at most 2 in a day. No soy yogurt, dairy, eggs, honey, fish sauce, oyster sauce or bonito.
- Nuts and seeds (almond, peanut, flax, kinako, black sesame): 25 slots, mostly inside the soy milk drinks and the congee.
- Groceries: 45 at Standard and Big, 44 at Light.
- Missing staples: mushroom oyster sauce and vegan fish sauce are not in the library (tamari, rice vinegar, sugar and lemongrass stand in); tteok, dried tangerine peel and zha cai (dropped for the grocery cap) are not used.
