# Diabetes-friendly South Asian: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, then revised after every lab run. This is the final week.

Places: Punjab (rajma, tandoori chicken tray, palak paneer, chicken do pyaza), Gujarat (methi thepla, tuvar dal with spinach and bhindi), Kerala (mackerel curry with a cabbage and carrot thoran, sundal), Pakistan (chana chaat, barley haleem, masala peanuts, anda chaat), Sri Lanka (pepper and tamarind salmon with parippu and a spinach mallung), Tamil Nadu and Karnataka (millet upma, kosambari, rasam).

Carbs: one modest, honest starch portion in each of the three meals, always next to dal, beans, egg, yogurt, fish or chicken. Brown basmati is a few spoonfuls (a Sunday pot, kept in the fridge and frozen in portions), roti is one a meal, millet and barley carry the breakfast upma and the haleem. No white rice, naan, maida, jaggery or juice. Carb style: whole grains too (brown basmati and whole-wheat atta, no refined staples).

Fish: Tuesday (Atlantic mackerel) and Friday (salmon). Three days apart, always cooked fresh, never a leftover.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Moong dal chilla with fenugreek and mint chutney (the classic plain cheela, no egg). Mon, Wed, Sat. Batter blended the night before.
- Methi besan thepla with curd. Tue, Fri. Dough the night before.
- Foxtail millet vegetable upma. Thu, Sun. Millet and chana dal soaked together the night before. (Upma sits on days when dinner has no mustard and curry leaf tempering of its own.)

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: Rajma masala with brown basmati and onion-tomato salad. 2 servings, Tuesday's lunch.
2. Tue: Kerala-style mackerel curry (coconut-free red tamarind gravy) with a cabbage and carrot thoran dressed in fresh coconut, and brown basmati. 1 serving, fresh.
3. Wed: Gujarati-style tuvar dal with spinach, bhindi and a roti. 2 servings, Thursday's lunch.
4. Thu: Tandoori chicken tray with roasted chickpeas, kachumber and mint raita (one roti). 2 servings, Friday's lunch.
5. Fri: Pepper and tamarind salmon with parippu and spinach mallung, a little brown basmati. 1 serving, fresh.
6. Sat (weekend project): Barley haleem with goat and dals. 2 servings, Sunday's lunch.
7. Sun: Palak paneer with a roti and cucumber-onion salad. 1 serving.

## Lunches cooked fresh
- Mon: Chana chaat with sprouted moong (no cook).
- Wed: Chicken do pyaza with a roti and cucumber salad.
- Sat: Lauki chana dal with a roti and onion salad.
- Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun: leftovers.

## Snacks (2 a day, Light keeps the first, Big adds a third)
Haldi doodh with almonds, masala boiled egg with carrot sticks, egg chaat, masala peanuts, cucumber and mint raita, guava chaat with almonds, oven-roasted masala chickpeas, sprouted moong kosambari, chickpea sundal. Big adds paneer tikka bites, moong dal shorba, toor dal rasam and more of the above.

## Light size
The Light week drops the second snack and makes two changes so every day can reach 1,400 to 1,600 kcal without a nutrient falling under 80% for women: Monday's lunch is the chicken do pyaza (B12 and B3 on a day with no fish or meat), and Tuesday keeps both its snacks (raita for calcium, boiled egg for choline). Rice and roti in Light are trimmed by the engine to as low as 5 to 8 g of rice and 11 to 18 g of flour at the 1,400 edge; I could not hold a floor there without breaking the GL gate on Monday.

## Check against GUIDE sections 11 and 12
- Cuisine fit: every dish is something a home cook in the region would put on the table. The tweaks are proportion (more dal, a smaller rice portion, a salad or vegetables first), never a different dish.
- Honest names: "Kerala-style", "Gujarati-style", "tandoori chicken tray" (oven, not tandoor), "inspired by ambul thiyal" for the salmon (the original uses tuna and goraka), "haleem" with barley doing most of the wheat's job, "Moong dal chilla" with an egg.
- Carbs are spread: each main meal has one starch portion with protein and fibre beside it; nothing is carb-only, and fruit comes with nuts or yogurt.
- Legumes at two meals on most days: chilla, chaat, rajma, tuvar dal, lauki chana dal, haleem, parippu, the chickpea tray, upma's chana dal.
- Acid and vegetables first: kachumber, raita, thoran and mallung come with the dinner, and lemon or tamarind is in every dish.
- No juice, no jaggery, no dried fruit. Tamarind does the sweet and sour.
- One flavour base a day. Upma sits on days when dinner has no mustard and curry leaf tempering of its own, and the chaat has no mint on the day the chilla has a mint chutney.
- Crutch counts (Standard): eggs 4 slots (boiled egg Mon and Tue, egg chaat Thu and Sun; none at breakfast), dairy 10 slots, nuts and seeds 12 slots (almonds and peanuts where the cuisine uses them, almonds on the tandoori tray, sunflower seeds in the thepla), fish 2.
- No mustard oil, flax or chia. Canola for everyday cooking, a little ghee on the dals.

## Final review fixes
- Light copy now says plainly that rice is "a spoonful or two" and the roti "a small roti, about the size of your palm" on the lighter sizes.
- Friday's salmon uses the rice frozen on Sunday; the mackerel, salmon, tuvar dal and mackerel steps no longer use an ingredient twice; the thepla takes a spoonful of curd, not two tablespoons; cook times corrected (tandoori 40, lauki 45, tuvar 50); untrue claims about millet and tuvar dal carbohydrate removed.
- Big Tuesday swaps the raita for masala peanuts, so that day holds about 330 g of yogurt instead of 710 g.

## What changed after the lab
- Rice dropped from about 50 g to 16 to 20 g a meal and roti to 20 to 24 g, because glycaemic load per 2,000 kcal has to stay under 70 once portions are scaled to 3,000 kcal.
- Chana palak became chicken do pyaza: the chickpeas pushed iron over the Big upper limit and left Wednesday short on protein.
- Coconut milk dropped for a little grated coconut (now all in the Tuesday thoran), and ghee is only a spoonful on the dals and the haleem, to keep saturated fat under 10%.
- After the choline estimate for pulses landed: plain moong cheela, no egg, upma without curd, eggs only as snacks.
- After the chef critic: upma soaked overnight with less chana dal; thepla with less methi and the salt last; Sunday brown basmati batch; the haleem finished with ghee; cucumber salads for the do pyaza and palak paneer; wording softened on rice and roti and on blood sugar; roti-dough tips; mustard and curry leaf tempering on the tuvar dal and parippu. Spinach stays in the chilla (cabbage-only broke vitamin A on Monday and Saturday), and sunflower seeds stay in the thepla (sesame dropped Light vitamin E under 80%).
- Light 1,200 to 1,400 still can't pass: five days will not trim below about 1,340 to 1,430 kcal without a woman's micronutrient falling under 80%.
