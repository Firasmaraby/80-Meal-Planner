# West African Pot: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, revised after the lab, and revised again after the chef critic. This is the final week.

Places: Nigeria (egg sauce, moi moi, akara, egusi, suya, pepper soup), Ghana (red-red, groundnut stew, roasted corn and groundnuts, orange and groundnuts), Senegal (chicken yassa, thieboudienne-inspired fish and rice, mafe-style stew), with a Cote d'Ivoire touch (poisson braise-inspired tilapia with cassava) and Sierra Leone-style stewed greens beside it.

Carbs: millet, yam, sweet potato, plantain, corn and black-eyed peas. Brown rice appears once (Saturday's thieb, cooked in the broth), so the week is "whole grains too" and otherwise whole-food.

Fish: smoked mackerel in Monday's egusi (leftover Tuesday lunch), and canned sardines with bones on warm yam on Friday and Sunday. Thursday's tilapia and Saturday's haddock are lean white fish, and the thieb fish is seared fresh on both days instead of reheated.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Egg sauce with collards and pan-grilled ripe plantain (sauce base made ahead). Mon, Thu, Sun.
- Moi moi with avocado and orange. Makes 2: Tue and Wed (soak Saturday night, steam Sunday evening, keeps 3 days).
- Spiced millet koko with yogurt, banana and groundnuts. Makes 2: Fri and Sat (cooked Thursday evening).

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: Egusi soup with smoked mackerel and bitter greens, cooked in red palm oil, with boiled yam. Makes 2, so Tuesday lunch.
2. Tue: Chicken yassa with steamed millet, grilled for char. Makes 2, so Wednesday lunch.
3. Wed: Groundnut stew with beef, sweet potato and collards. Makes 2, so Thursday lunch.
4. Thu: Grilled tilapia with onion and chili sauce, boiled cassava and stewed greens (poisson braise-inspired). 1 serving.
5. Fri: Red-red, black-eyed pea stew in palm oil with pan-fried ripe plantain, egg and avocado. Makes 2, so Saturday lunch.
6. Sat (weekend project): Thieboudienne-inspired haddock and vegetable rice with brown rice, ladled with broth. Makes 2, so Sunday lunch, with the second fillet seared fresh.
7. Sun: Goat and yam pepper soup with collards, spiced with black pepper, nutmeg, clove and allspice. 1 serving.

## Lunches
- Mon: Nigerian-style salad with black-eyed peas, corn and avocado (peas cooked on Sunday).
- Fri: Suya-spiced beef (flank, with a paprika, cayenne, garlic, onion and ginger yaji) with a cucumber and collard slaw and sweet potato.
- Tue, Wed, Thu, Sat, Sun: leftovers.

## Snacks (2 a day, Light drops the second)
West African roadside snacks: orange and groundnuts, groundnut and sesame bites, roasted corn with fresh coconut, akara (twice), sardines and tomato on warm yam (twice), avocado with tomato and lime, plain roasted groundnuts. Only two yogurt snacks (orange yogurt with toasted sesame, Mon and Sat), plus the yogurt in koko. Big adds boiled egg with tomato and pepper, banana and groundnuts, groundnut and sesame bites, plain groundnuts and fresh coconut with groundnuts.

## Checks against GUIDE section 11
- Ingredients not of the cuisine: none added for a number. Collards and mustard greens stand in for ugwu and bitterleaf, melon seeds are egusi, dried shrimp stands in for crayfish (kept to five dishes), smoked mackerel stands in for smoked fish, and chili stands in for scotch bonnet (the label says so).
- Dish renamed into something it isn't: thieb, yassa, koko, suya and poisson braise all say "inspired" where we changed the starch or the protein.
- Breakfasts: egg sauce with plantain, moi moi and koko are everyday West African breakfasts.
- Grocery cap: dried spices and salt no longer count toward the 45, so goat, cassava, carrot and a splash of vinegar are back, along with black pepper, ground ginger and onion powder.
- Same flavour base twice in a day: egusi and yassa no longer use a tomato base, the fresh sauces are lighter, and each day mixes a tomato-pepper dish with a spice, onion or clear-broth dish. Some tomato and pepper overlap is the cuisine.
- One vegetable everywhere: collards, mustard greens, cabbage, carrot, cucumber and avocado are rotated, spinach is gone, and plantain is kept to small portions (60 to 70 g) across its four meals.
- Protein crutches: no yogurt dressing on the salad, no eggs on the salad, and yogurt only in koko and two snacks.
- Lists match the steps: raw weights for meat, fish, yam, cassava, plantain, sweet potato, millet, rice and dry peas. The only cooked-ahead items are the Monday salad's peas and the moi moi, and the steps say how.
- Omega-6 comes from groundnuts, melon seeds and palm oil in honest amounts, and the week stays well under 6:1 with room to spare.
