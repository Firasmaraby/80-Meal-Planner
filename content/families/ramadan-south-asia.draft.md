# Ramadan South Asian: draft week

Places: Pakistan (haleem, chicken karahi, chana chaat, dahi fruit chaat, pakora, kheer, shakarkandi chaat), North India (daliya, masala oats, bhurji paratha, dal tarka, haldi doodh), Bangladesh (shorshe-style salmon, mackerel jhol).

Halal: no pork, alcohol or gelatin. Meat is goat (haleem) and chicken (karahi), with a "buy halal" tip on both. No Rooh Afza: the library has no rose syrup, and a sugar syrup drink does not fit the plan.

Fava beans: not used, so no favism line.

## Suhoor (3 recipes, every one with dahi and fruit, salt kept at 0.5 g)
- Palak anda bhurji with a lighter whole-wheat paratha (days 1, 4). Eggs cooked until firm.
- Palak daliya with moong and peanuts, dahi, orange (days 2, 5, 7). Soak the moong the night before.
- Masala oats with a firm boiled egg, dahi and apple (days 3, 6).

## Iftar, two dinner entries a day
Opener (dates and water, plus a starter that carries 20 g of protein, because the gate counts every dinner entry):
- Dahi fruit chaat of orange, papaya and pomegranate (days 1, 4), chana chaat (3, 5), baked carrot and onion besan pakora with mint dahi (2, 6), sprouted moong and egg chaat (7, store-bought sprouts boiled a full 4 minutes, eaten within 1 hour).

Plate:
1. Goat haleem with barley, cracked wheat, moong and lentils, a roti and carrot salad. Makes 2, leftover day 2. Weekend-project length, freezable.
2. Salmon in a mustard jhol with brown basmati and garlic spinach. Fresh.
3. Masoor palak dal with vegetable pulao and cucumber raita. Makes 2, leftover day 5.
4. Mackerel jhol with potato, peas and a roti. Fresh.
5. Chicken karahi with roti and kachumber. Fresh.

Fish: day 3 salmon, day 6 mackerel.

## Snacks (2 a day; Light drops the second; Big adds a third on days 1, 2, 4, 7)
Mango lassi, guava with almonds, papaya with almonds, daliya kheer (twice, the one sweet), egg masala (twice), haldi doodh (twice), roasted chickpeas, masala peanuts and carrot sticks. Big adds shakarkandi chaat (days 1 and 7), carrot sticks with peanuts (day 2) and papaya with almonds (day 4), so no Big day has four dairy slots.

## Notes from the lab
- Every dinner entry needs 15 g of protein, so the openers carry chickpeas, dahi, besan or egg, not just fruit.
- Vitamin E and the omega ratio were the hard parts on the haleem day: canola for the browning, spinach in the daliya, almonds in the snacks.
- Glycemic load: dates kept to about 30 g, the kheer is cracked wheat with 5 g of sugar, no banana.
- Dairy is on 3 slots on most days (yogurt-forward cuisine, never 4).
- 45 groceries at Big, 44 at Standard.

## After the critic review
- Safety: haleem, daliya and kheer chill within 1 hour; pulses and grains soak in the fridge; haleem has a 71 C check and a start-by-mid-afternoon line; sprouts are store-bought, boiled 4 minutes and eaten within 1 hour.
- Chef: cucumber at most twice a day, fruit chaat has no guava or apple, pakora is carrot and onion (no second spinach dish on days 2 and 6), salmon kachumber is carrot and tomato.
- Salt: haleem 2.0 g, dal 1.8 g, salmon 1.6 g. Max sodium is 1,867 mg at Light and 2,423 mg at Big 3200, well under the ceiling. Suhoor stays at 0.5 g.
- Dates: "a date, or about three small dates (30 g)".

## After the final review
- Pakora is now an onion (piyaz) pakora, the snack sticks are mooli, and the fruit chaat uses pear, so carrot is at most 2 slots a day and Big day 4 has papaya only once. Lab passes at every edge.
- Safety text: roasted chana kept in the fridge, haleem second portion packed before sitting down, oat soak in the fridge, sprouts "boiled a full 4 minutes", dried chickpeas soaked in the fridge, mackerel hand-washing line moved to the raw-fish step, pearl barley named, pressure-cook option for the haleem grains.
- Overclaims removed: the shakarkandi chaat is "a winter street classic" and the salmon jhol is "sharp with mustard".
