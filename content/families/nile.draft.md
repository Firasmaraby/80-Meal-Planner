# Nile Valley Kitchen: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, then revised after the lab and the chef critic. This is the final week.

Places: Cairo (molokhia with chicken, koshari, ful medames, shakshuka masri, bamia bil lahma), Alexandria (grilled sardines, sayadeya with mackerel, kebda Iskandarani, eggs with pastirma), Sudan (Sudanese-style ful with white cheese, chicken and peanut mullah with sorghum asida, dates with tahini). No Saidi dish fits the week, so Upper Egypt is not claimed.

Carbs: lentils, chickpeas, fava, sweet potato, baladi bread (whole-wheat pita), brown rice, a little whole-wheat pasta, sorghum. No white rice or white pasta, so this is a "whole grains too" week. Bread and rice are kept modest because fava, brown rice and pita all have a high glycaemic index.

Fat: olive oil for the cooking, tahini and sesame, peanuts in the mullah, dukkah (curated) as a finish on eggs and as a dip. No canola, flax, chia, walnuts or loose hazelnuts. Ghee and sesame oil are named in tips only.

Fish: every day, because a tahini, sesame and peanut kitchen runs high in omega-6 and one fish dinner is not enough to keep each day under 9:1. Mon lunch (tinned sardines in baladi bread), Tue breakfast (ful with tinned sardines), Wed dinner (fresh grilled sardines), Thu lunch (tinned mackerel in tomato), Fri snack (sardines with tomato and shatta on bread), Sat dinner (mackerel sayadeya) and Sun lunch (the sayadeya, reheated). The two fresh-fish dinners are Wednesday and Saturday, never back to back. Friday breakfast is a ful with egg, not sardines, to break the run.

## Breakfasts
- Mon: shakshuka masri, eggs in garlicky tomato and red pepper, finished with dukkah.
- Tue: ful medames with tinned sardines, tahini, lemon and cucumber.
- Wed: eggs with basterma-style pastrami and tomato, with olives and dukkah.
- Thu: Sudanese-style ful with an egg, white cheese (gebna beida), tomato, sesame and rocket.
- Fri: ful medames with tahini, lemon and a boiled egg.
- Sat: baked green ta'meya with cucumber and rocket salad, tahini and olives (2 portions, weekend project).
- Sun: Saturday's ta'meya, re-crisped.

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon: molokhia with chicken and brown rice. 2 portions, Tuesday lunch.
2. Tue: koshari reimagined, lentils and brown rice with chickpeas and a little whole-wheat pasta. 2 portions, Wednesday lunch.
3. Wed: Alexandrian grilled sardines with tahini sauce and a cucumber, tomato and carrot salad. 1 portion.
4. Thu: bamia bil lahma with brown rice. 2 portions, Friday lunch.
5. Fri: Sudanese chicken and peanut mullah with spinach and sorghum asida. 2 portions, Saturday lunch (with pita or rice, since asida does not keep).
6. Sat (weekend project): Alexandrian sayadeya, mackerel over caramelized onion and cumin brown rice with a few toasted almonds. 2 portions, Sunday lunch.
7. Sun: kebda Iskandarani, a small portion of chicken liver with garlic, cumin and sweet pepper in baladi bread, with sweet potato wedges and chickpeas.

## Other lunches
- Mon: tinned sardine, tomato and rocket in baladi bread with tahini, lemon and dried mint.
- Thu: tinned mackerel in garlicky tomato with salata baladi and baladi bread.

## Snacks (1 or 2 a day, each recipe at most twice)
Orange with dates, hummus el-sham (warm cumin chickpeas), a carrot, orange and almond smoothie, zabadi with dates, zabadi bil khiar, roasted sweet potato, sardines with tomato and shatta on bread, dates with tahini, gebna beida with olives and mint, termis. Big adds a snack a day. Light drops the second snack each day, and Sunday's only snack.

## Check against GUIDE section 11
- Not of the cuisine: no chia, flax, walnuts, canola or loose hazelnuts. Peanuts are Sudanese, tahini and sesame are everywhere, dukkah is Egyptian.
- Renamed dishes: koshari says "reimagined". Basterma is beef pastrami, said in the recipe. Ful with white cheese is "Sudanese-style", not "ful Sudani" (which means peanuts). The sorghum asida is the real asida.
- Protein crutches: eggs only where the dish has them (shakshuka, basterma-style pastrami, two ful), 4 breakfasts. Dairy is white cheese in one ful, a yogurt sauce on the liver, and a few yogurt or cheese snacks.
- Same base twice in a day: tomato is Egypt's base, so raw tomato is kept to a few dishes and cucumber, carrot, rocket and olives carry the others.
- One vegetable everywhere: rocket in 5 recipes, cucumber in 4 meals and 2 snacks, tomato in most Egyptian dishes. Okra, jute, spinach, carrot, sweet potato and red pepper are spread across the week.
- Liver: 45 g of chicken liver once a week, well under the vitamin A upper limit, with a note to skip it in pregnancy.
- Missing from the library: baladi bread (whole-wheat pita used), basterma (beef pastrami used), kisra (sorghum asida, or pita), dried okra (not used).
