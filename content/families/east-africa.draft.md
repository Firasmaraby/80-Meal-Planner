## GUIDE section 11 check

- Not of the cuisine: dried maize is the real githeri grain. Peanuts (karanga) are used in Kenya and Tanzania, kolo and
  nifro are Ethiopian, liver is natural in Somali suqaar ("beer"). No flax, chia or walnuts. Niter kibbeh, mitmita and
  xawaash are the real staples. Oil is canola, with sunflower oil (the region's own) on the mishkaki, samaki and
  sardine days.
- Renamed dishes: garam masala stands in for the Swahili spice mix, cottage cheese for ayib, chickpea flour plus berbere
  for shiro powder. All noted.
- Protein crutches: eggs only where the dish has them (sukuma wiki, ful, doro wat), 6 slots. Liver only in suqaar.
- Same flavour base twice in a day: Saturday's lunch is Somali suqaar and dinner is Ethiopian doro wat. Thursday has a
  fresh mishkaki lunch and shiro dinner. Berbere shows up at one meal a day at most.
- One vegetable everywhere: avocado in 4 slots (azifa, two kachumbari snacks, mishkaki kachumbari), none in mayai,
  ful or githeri. Kale in sukuma wiki and samaki, collards in gomen, doro and suqaar, spinach in githeri and azifa.
- Weird breakfasts: ful, eggs with greens and uji are all breakfast food in the region.
- Batch staples: githeri says soak overnight, pressure-cook, keeps four days. Sorghum is cooked the evening before the
  suqaar and keeps four days. Uji says how to batch it. Kolo says how long it keeps.
- Slot counts (Standard): dairy 9 (milk or cottage cheese, niter kibbeh not counted), egg 6, liver 2 (one pot), nut or
  seed 6.
