# Thai & Vietnamese Fresh Kitchen: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, then revised after the first lab runs (the calcium, omega and glycaemic load gates moved a few dishes around). Checked against GUIDE section 11.

Places: central Thailand (red curry, steamed lime fish, khai jiao, jok), Isaan (larb neua, grilled fish with som tam), northern Vietnam (phở bò, đậu phụ sốt cà chua) and southern Vietnam (gỏi cuốn, cơm tấm with lemongrass pork, cá kho tộ, xôi). Both countries show up through the week and every region at least twice.

Carbs: one big pot of brown rice on Sunday and a second on Wednesday night, black rice for the xôi, and refined rice products in only two dishes: rice noodles in the phở and rice paper in the gỏi cuốn. That makes the honest label "everything in moderation", but the week is mostly brown and black rice, mung beans and a lot of vegetables.

Fish: Monday (salmon steamed with lime, garlic and chili), Friday (whole lemongrass mackerel with som tam) and Sunday (cá thu kho tộ). Never back to back, always cooked fresh.

Fat: one neutral oil (canola, the usual "vegetable oil" in a Canadian kitchen) for the pan, a little coconut milk in the curry, the xôi and the chè. No flax, no chia, no yogurt.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Jok moo: Thai pork and ginger rice porridge with a soft egg and gai lan, from the batch rice. Mon, Wed, Fri.
- Khai jiao: Thai pork omelette with tomato, cilantro and brown rice. Tue, Thu.
- Xôi: black glutinous rice with mung beans, a soft egg, dried shrimp, peanut and a cup of warm soy milk. Sat, makes 2, so Sunday is a reheat.

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon (central Thailand): steamed salmon with lime, garlic and chili, water spinach and carrot. 1 serving.
2. Tue (Isaan): larb neua, beef herb salad in lettuce cups. 2 servings, makes Wednesday's lunch.
3. Wed (southern Vietnam): thịt nướng sả, lemongrass pork on brown rice with pickles, water spinach, scallion oil and nước chấm. 2 servings, makes Thursday's lunch.
4. Thu (central Thailand): red curry with chicken, tofu and gai lan. 2 servings, makes Friday's lunch.
5. Fri (Isaan): whole lemongrass mackerel with som tam. 1 serving.
6. Sat (northern Vietnam, the weekend project): phở bò with a slow broth and a herb plate. 2 servings, Sunday's lunch.
7. Sun (southern Vietnam): cá thu kho tộ, caramel mackerel with water spinach and carrot. 1 serving.

## Lunches

## Check against GUIDE section 11
- Not of the cuisine: peanuts, dried shrimp, tofu, soy milk and mung beans are everyday Thai and Vietnamese ingredients. Nothing is added only for a number. No flax, chia, walnuts or yogurt.
- Renamed dishes: the salmon "pla neung manao", the mackerel kho and the grilled mackerel all say "inspired by", and brown rice stands in for sticky rice in the larb and the grilled fish (said so). Thai basil stands in for holy basil in the pad kra pao (said so).
- Protein crutches: 8 egg slots in egg dishes (jok, khai jiao, pad kra pao, xôi). The cơm tấm plate and the kho no longer have eggs. Choline on the weekend days needs them.
- Same flavour base twice in a day: lemongrass appears in the curry paste, the steamed salmon, the pork marinade, the grilled mackerel and the phở, never twice in a day. The lime and chili sauces differ each day.
- One vegetable everywhere: gai lan is only in the jok and the curry. Water spinach is the cooked green with the salmon, the pork plate and the kho, and yardlong beans replace green beans in the larb, som tam and pad kra pao.
- Weird breakfasts: none. Rice porridge, a Thai omelette and xôi are all breakfasts at home.
- Lists match steps: raw weights for anything cooked, the black rice and brown rice are the only cooked grains, the whole mackerel is listed at its edible weight and says so.
- Silent sub-recipes: the curry paste, nam jim, nước chấm, pickle, caramel, toasted rice powder and ginger syrup are all made inside the steps.
- Fish sauce is measured in every recipe (1 to 6 g per serving), iodized salt replaces some of it in the breakfasts and tofu dish, and lime, chili, ginger, lemongrass and herbs carry the rest of the flavour.
