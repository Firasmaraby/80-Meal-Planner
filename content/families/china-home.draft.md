# Chinese Home Cooking: draft week (names and one-line ideas)

Written before any grams, then checked against GUIDE section 11.

Places: Sichuan (mapo tofu, doubanjiang mackerel), Cantonese (oyster-sauce gai lan, lotus root and chicken, congee, the Sunday steamed fish), Shanghai (red-braised beef and daikon, scallion oil salmon) and the north (millet porridge, zhajiangmian, pork and napa dumplings, egg pancake). Every region shows up at least twice.

Carbs: brown rice cooked with pearl barley (a mixed-grain rice, the way many kitchens stretch rice) in one big batch on Sunday and again on Wednesday night, whole-wheat noodles, whole-wheat dumpling wrappers and pancakes, millet porridge. Barley keeps the glycaemic load in range. Labelled "whole grains too"; whole-wheat flour (not cornstarch) thickens the sauces so no refined starch sneaks in.

Fish: Tuesday (scallion oil salmon), Thursday (doubanjiang braised mackerel) and the Sunday steamed sea bass. The two fatty fish nights are never back to back and are always cooked fresh, never leftovers.

Oil: one neutral wok oil (canola, which is what "vegetable oil" is in Canada) for the wok, and toasted sesame oil by the drop at the end. No flax, no chia.

## Breakfasts (3 recipes)
- Brown rice and barley congee with shrimp, a soft egg, bok choy and walnuts. Mon, Wed, Fri. 15 minutes from the Sunday rice.
- Whole-wheat scallion egg pancake with cucumber and a glass of warm soy milk. Tue, Thu. 10 minutes.
- Millet and sweet potato porridge with a soy-braised egg and tofu, walnuts and quick-pickled daikon. Sat, makes 2, so Sunday is a reheat.

## Dinners and the lunches they make
1. Mon (Sichuan): Mapo tofu with shiitake and pork, garlicky gai lan and brown rice. 2 servings, makes Tuesday's lunch.
2. Tue (Shanghai and Canton): Pan-seared salmon with ginger scallion oil, bok choy and brown rice. 1 serving.
3. Wed (Canton): Chicken with snow peas, shiitake, carrot and almonds, over mixed rice. 2 servings, makes Thursday's lunch.
4. Thu (Sichuan): Braised mackerel and tofu in doubanjiang sauce, with cold garlic bok choy. 1 serving.
5. Fri (Shanghai): Red-braised beef shank with daikon, carrots, braised eggs and napa. 2 servings, makes Saturday's lunch.
6. Sat (north, the weekend project): Pork and napa dumplings in whole-wheat wrappers, with a black vinegar dip and smashed cucumber. 2 servings, Sunday's lunch.
7. Sun (Canton): Whole steamed sea bass over tofu with ginger and scallion, gai lan and mixed rice. 1 serving.

## Lunches
- Mon: Plain tomato and egg stir-fry (three eggs, no tofu) over mixed rice.
- Wed: Egg fried brown rice with tofu and edamame (cold batch rice is what makes fried rice good).
- Fri: Zhajiangmian-inspired whole-wheat noodles with pork and mushroom sauce, cucumber, carrot and daikon.
- Tue, Thu, Sat, Sun: leftovers.

## Snacks (2 a day, Light drops the second)
Mandarin with peanuts, walnuts or almonds, roasted sweet potato, warm soy milk with walnuts, Asian pear with almonds or walnuts, cold tofu with scallion and soy, edamame with sansho salt, smashed cucumber with peanuts, a soy-braised egg. Index 0 of each day carries the nutrient that matters (almonds for vitamin E, soy milk or tofu for calcium) so Light keeps it. Big adds a third snack each day from the same shelf.

## Check against GUIDE section 11
- Not of the cuisine: nothing added only for a number. Peanuts, sesame, walnuts and almonds are all everyday Chinese snacks and toppings (walnut congee, almond chicken). No flax or chia.
- Renamed dishes: the sansho stands in for Sichuan peppercorn (said so), whole-wheat pasta stands in for whole-wheat noodles (said so), and dishes that depart say "inspired by".
- Protein crutches: eggs are in dishes that are egg dishes (tomato and egg, pancake, congee, fried rice, braised egg). No egg tacked on a stir-fry.
- Same flavour base twice in a day: doubanjiang is Monday dinner and Thursday dinner, never in the same day as another paste. 
- One vegetable everywhere: gai lan (Mon, Sun), bok choy (congee, Tue, Thu), napa (Fri, Sat). Cucumber in three, shiitake in three.
- Weird breakfasts: none. Congee, egg pancake and millet porridge are all breakfasts in China.
- Lists match steps: rice from the batch is the only cooked grain; everything else cooked is raw weight. Whole fish is listed at its edible weight and says so. The beef braise lists its hard-boiled eggs.
- Untrue claims: calcium claims only for the calcium-set tofu dishes, which say to check the label.
- Silent sub-recipes: the dumpling dough, dips, scallion oil and braising liquid are all made inside the steps.
